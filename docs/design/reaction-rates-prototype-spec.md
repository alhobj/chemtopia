# Reaction rates — chips and powder

## Authority and first increment

The owner requested “Implement number 4. Reaction rates” after the proposal to react calcium carbonate chips or powder with excess HCl, collect CO₂ in a gas syringe, and compare volume–time curves, early rates and final yields. This authorises the bounded tenth local lab. Preserve the selected B equipment direction, laboratory-room entry, shared drawer, gravity/friction, solid contacts, deliberate actions, possible mistakes and numerical review. No accounts, persistence, deployment or cross-lab notebook is included.

## Investigation and controls

Students choose carbonate form and mass, acid volume, assembly and measurement timing. Both solids and all required apparatus are supplied. The spatula takes **0.10 g** or **0.01 g** model-measured portions, then delivers them into the open reaction flask. This abstracts weighing into explicit measured doses; it is not a claim that a real scoop automatically weighs those masses. Liquid additions are finite **10 mL** or **1 mL**, with no continuing stream or millisecond stopping challenge.

A convenient comparison is **0.200 g** of chips versus **0.200 g** of powder with **10.0 mL of 1.00 M HCl**, both at the fixed model temperature of 20 °C. These numerical defaults are implementation examples, not additional owner answers or an IB-prescribed recipe. They give excess acid and approximately 48 mL theoretical CO₂ in a 100 mL syringe. Other amounts, mixtures, dilution and acid shortage remain possible.

The supplied inner acid vial keeps acid physically separate from solid in the flask. Fill the vial, fit it inside the flask, clamp the syringe near its stand, fit the bung and connect the delivery tube. Tipping the vial transfers the liquid deliberately. If the clock has not been started, the primary mix action can start it at the same time; independent clock and mix-only controls still permit timing mistakes. Students can also pour acid directly onto the solid and start a reaction before sealing the apparatus. The task does not require racing to replace a bung.

The gas syringe has an inlet, piston, true 0–100 mL graduations and a live numerical reading. Read the piston face nearest the inlet. Its clamp uses the existing physical joint/collision system and leaves a real syringe collision body. The bung and inner vial use contained attachment exceptions in the flask. The flexible delivery tube connects the bung to a nearby syringe; open, removed, stored or overextended connections prevent collection. Students can loosen/tighten a connector and empty the syringe, losing gas rather than resetting the reaction.

## Reaction and balances

CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g).

Track both carbonate forms through sources, spatula, vessels and spills. Reaction extent consumes carbonate and two HCl equivalents per CO₂ molecule. The limiting reactant bounds total production. Generated gas is accounted for as collected or escaped, including capacity overflow, deliberate emptying and leaks. Carbonate equivalents, acid equivalents and CO₂ are checked quantitatively; the carrier solution volume is conserved as a dilute-volume approximation, rather than tracking molecular water formation or a full density/product-speciation model.

A shrinking-particle teaching law uses remaining-solid fraction to the power 2/3, with relative rate proportional to remaining acid concentration. Chip and powder coefficients are respectively **0.055 s⁻¹** and **0.180 s⁻¹**, applied to initial carbonate equivalents. These coefficients are uncalibrated illustrative choices, not measured reaction-rate data or an experimentally established rate law. Mixed solids remain finite when the powder fraction runs out. Additional reactants after reaction begins are retained in the charged-amount record and flagged as a changed experiment.

Use M(CaCO₃) = **100.09 g mol⁻¹** and a supplied rounded ambient molar gas volume of **24 000 mL mol⁻¹**. The 100 mL syringe vents model overflow rather than simulating pressure buildup or damage. A loose connector captures 35% of newly produced gas and loses retained gas with a **0.060 s⁻¹** illustrative leakage coefficient. Correctly connected equipment collects all model product gas; matched pure-form experiments therefore have the same final theoretical yield despite different rates.

Wrong-vessel and spill reactions also consume reagents and produce gas. A reacting vial inside a sealed, connected flask can vent through its headspace; other unconnected pools lose their gas. Detailed gas dissolution, headspace displacement/dead-volume filling, pressure, compressibility, heating/cooling and calibrated particle-size distributions are outside this increment. The syringe uses an illustrative internal piston/actuator drawing, not a detailed mechanical plunger simulation. Bubble animation indicates reaction qualitatively, not a calibrated bubble-volume count.

## Measurements, trials and graph

The stopwatch is independent of the chemistry: stopping it does not stop reaction or gas collection. Record displayed time to **0.1 s** and syringe volume to **0.1 mL** manually, or explicitly enable the **two-second logger**. Records with the same displayed time but different volumes remain visible, exposing a stopped-clock mistake; exact duplicate readings are omitted.

The plot uses recorded data only, connects observations and labels time in seconds and collected CO₂ in millilitres. Saved trial curves have separate colours and a legend. A current trial keeps updating after Save. **Fresh trial** saves available observations and supplies fresh apparatus while retaining previous trials in memory. **Restart** discards the whole attempt. Reloading or leaving the lab does not save observations to a file, browser storage or a server.

Trial summaries show charged masses, acid volume/concentration and the early two-point mean collection rate. A matched comparison check requires equal masses and acid conditions, different pure forms, initially sealed collection, an initially running clock, and no later additions or stopped-clock interval. It is a basic conditions check, not a guarantee of complete or accurate final data; students must inspect duration, later leaks and the full curves.

The early secant slope estimates an early mean collection rate. It is explicitly **not an exact initial tangent** or an automatically fitted kinetic rate constant. A dedicated tangent/best-fit-curve tool remains a later refinement; this first increment does not claim full coverage of every syllabus graph-analysis requirement.

## Finish and questions

Finish freezes this attempt and retains saved observations. Three questions with retry use balanced shuffled concept choices and typed numerical answers:

1. Explain why increased exposed surface permits more frequent effective collisions, without changing the reactant amount or particle energy.
2. Calculate mean collection rate from two recorded readings and their time interval. Incomplete data uses an explicitly labelled supplied example.
3. Calculate maximum CO₂ from the recorded charge (or labelled example), using the 2:1 HCl/CaCO₃ stoichiometry and supplied molar gas volume. Account for the limiting reagent.

Observed readings, leakage and mistakes are not replaced with ideal values. A higher early rate does not establish a higher final yield. Numerical review completion is distinct from proving a fair, complete practical comparison.
